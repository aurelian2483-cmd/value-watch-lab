| Renewal strong law | Robert G. Gallager, MIT OCW 6.262, *Discrete Stochastic Processes*, Chapter 4, §4.3, Theorem 4.3.1. [Official MIT OCW PDF](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-262-discrete-stochastic-processes-spring-2011/931ffa0940899c27f34b71ad64fd2bb0_MIT6_262S11_chap04.pdf) | The theorem states (N(t)/t\to1/\bar X) a.s. for finite mean inter-renewal interval. The same chapter explicitly says the theorem also holds when the mean is infinite, by truncation. In that case the limiting rate is zero. | The correction making the finite-mean condition explicit is right, but the catalogue should also record the infinite-mean extension and its zero limiting rate rather than imply the finite-mean statement is the whole theorem. |
| ISTA / proximal L1 / FISTA | Amir Beck and Marc Teboulle (2009), *A Fast Iterative Shrinkage-Thresholding Algorithm for Linear Inverse Problems*, SIAM Journal on Imaging Sciences 2(1), 183–202. [Publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1137/080716542) · [Author-hosted paper](https://www.tau.ac.il/~becka/FISTA.pdf) | The paper defines ISTA as a gradient step followed by the proximal/shrinkage operator (including the constant-step version). FISTA evaluates that operator at an extrapolated point built from the previous iterates, with a separate momentum recurrence. | Supports retaining `prox-l1` and `ista` as separate catalogue IDs but recording conditional equivalence only when objective, penalty mask, initialization and step size coincide. FISTA is a distinct iteration because of extrapolation. |
| IPW / marginal structural models | James M. Robins, Miguel A. Hernán and Babette Brumback (2000), *Marginal Structural Models and Causal Inference in Epidemiology*, Epidemiology 11, 550–560. [Original paper PDF](https://epiresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/Robins_EPI_2000_11_550.pdf) | The paper estimates causal effects of time-varying exposure/treatment using longitudinal covariate history and outcomes observed over follow-up. It is retrospective causal estimation, not a source of an as-of pre-event feature for the same target outcome. | Retain the assessment-only / retrospective timing restriction for target-event causal estimators. A future prediction task must use only information available before its own outcome and must define a separate estimand. |
| Conway–Maxwell–Poisson (CMP) | Kimberly F. Sellers, Andrew W. Swift and Kimberly S. Weems (2017), *A flexible distribution class for count data*, Journal of Statistical Distributions and Applications 4, article 22. [Open-access article](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40488-017-0077-0) | The paper gives (P(X=x\mid\lambda,\nu)=\lambda^x/((x!)^\nu Z(\lambda,\nu))), with (Z=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\lambda^j/(j!)^\nu). Applying the ratio test gives convergence for finite \(\lambda\ge0\) when \(\nu>0\); for \(\nu=0\), the geometric series converges only for \(0\le\lambda<1\). For \(\lambda>0,\nu<0\), terms do not yield a finite normalizer. \(\lambda=0\) is the point mass at zero under the usual convention. | Catalogue domain note is mathematically supported; make the \(\nu<0\) exclusion explicit and state the zero-rate convention. This convergence-domain conclusion is our derivation from the published series, not a quotation of a parameter-domain theorem from the article. |
| Nelder–Mead | J. A. Nelder and R. Mead (1965), *A simplex method for function minimization*, The Computer Journal 7(4), 308–313. [Original paper scan](https://web.tecnico.ulisboa.pt/~mcasquilho/culture/cabinets/bookcase/Nelder-Mead.pdf) | The original algorithm describes reflection, expansion and contraction; when contraction fails it shrinks the simplex toward the best point. Its acceptance conditions are ordered by the reflected objective value relative to the best and worst vertices. | Supports correcting incomplete branch/acceptance descriptions. Do not collapse the acceptance cases into an informal “reflect, else contract” rule. |
| Skellam score difference | J. G. Skellam (1946), *The Frequency Distribution of the Difference between Two Poisson Variates Belonging to Different Populations*, JRSS Series A 109(3), 296. [Publisher record / DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2397-2335.1946.tb04670.x) | The original result concerns the difference of two Poisson variates. The shared-additive-component qualification follows exactly by algebra: if (X=X_1+C) and (Y=Y_1+C), then (X-Y=X_1-Y_1); the common component cancels. If the residual counts are independent Poisson, their difference has the Skellam law. | The catalogue correction is sound when it says a common additive Poisson shock cancels. It must not imply that arbitrary dependence between residual components is covered by the standard independent-Poisson Skellam result. |
