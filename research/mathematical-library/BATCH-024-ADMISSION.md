- BFGS: [FitBenchmarking equations](https://fitbenchmarking.readthedocs.io/en/latest/users/algorithms/line_search.html) confirm inverse update and direction. Positive curvature preserves positive definiteness; a line search alone does not necessarily establish curvature. Distinct from scalar Newton root and fixed-gradient updates.
- Gauss–Newton: [FitBenchmarking normal equations](https://fitbenchmarking.readthedocs.io/en/latest/users/algorithms/line_search.html) and [Cornell nonlinear least squares](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/courses/cs4220/2026sp/lec/2026-04-10.html) support the residual Jacobian approximation. Existing SVD least squares solves a fixed linear system; it can be a component of this iterative procedure.
- Levenberg–Marquardt: [Cornell trust regions](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/courses/cs4220/2026sp/lec/2026-04-20.html) confirms isotropic damping and actual/model reduction safeguards. Damping changes direction generally, not just length. No global minimum guarantee.
- Cyclic exact coordinate minimization: [Wright 2015 abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04759) and [Wright and Lee 2020 indexed abstract](https://doi.org/10.1090/MCOM/3530) describe coordinate minimization and cyclic exact updates; the latter identifies equivalence to Gauss–Seidel for the corresponding SPD quadratic system. The general scalar-subproblem procedure is not another count for the linear special case. No universal convergence claim. Full paper not retrieved.
- Dogleg: [Cornell trust regions, inexact solves](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/courses/cs4220/2026sp/lec/2026-04-20.html) confirms all three positive-definite boundary cases. At zero gradient stop separately. Not an exact general trust-region solver.
- Armijo backtracking: [Cornell line search](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/courses/cs4220/2026sp/lec/2026-04-17.html) confirms geometric trials and sufficient decrease under strict descent. A reusable step-selection algorithm, not fixed-step gradient descent or a curvature criterion.
